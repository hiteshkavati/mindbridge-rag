## Two things at once
Pressure from parents or relatives is often a mix of care, worry and social comparison. You can respect the care without accepting every demand. Separating the concern underneath ("they worry about my security") from the specific demand ("take this exact job") makes it easier to respond.

## Choose the conversation
Pick a calm time and start by acknowledging their worry. Then share your plan with specifics: what you are doing, by when, and how you will handle the risk they are afraid of. Concrete steps reassure more than general statements like "trust me".

## Hold some limits
It is fine to say "I will update you every month, but I do not want to discuss this at every family gathering". Repeat it calmly. Boundaries usually need many repetitions.

## Find an ally
A sibling, aunt, uncle or family friend who understands you can speak to elders in ways you cannot.

## Expect guilt
Guilt about disappointing family is normal and does not mean your decision is wrong. If pressure turns into threats, control or harassment, seek help from someone outside the family, such as a counsellor.
