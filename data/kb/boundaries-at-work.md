## Why it feels hard
Saying no at work can feel risky, especially early in your career or in a small team. The trick is to frame it as a trade-off, not a refusal.

## Phrases that work
"I can take this on if the report moves to next Thursday. Which should I prioritise?" "I am at capacity this week. I can start on Monday, or I can hand over X to make room." Managers usually respond better to concrete options than to "I am overwhelmed".

## After-hours messages
Decide what your norm is (for example, replying the next morning unless it is an agreed on-call situation) and apply it consistently. Turning off notifications outside work hours is a simple start. Clear expectations protect you more than occasional heroics.

## Start small
Practise on low-stakes requests first. Keep a short log of what you are handling so conversations about priorities are based on facts.

## If pushing back changes nothing
Some cultures punish boundaries. Keep a record, escalate to HR or a skip-level manager if it is a policy issue, and start thinking about options if the pattern stays the same.
