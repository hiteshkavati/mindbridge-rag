## High standards versus perfectionism
High standards motivate you and allow some mistakes. Perfectionism ties your worth to flawless results, so a small error feels like a personal failure. It often leads to endless checking, delay in starting or finishing, and burnout.

## Define good enough
Before you start, decide what "good enough" looks like for this task and how long it deserves. Not everything needs your best effort; choose where it matters. Time-box the work and stop when the time is up.

## Ship earlier
Share drafts and ask for feedback sooner than feels comfortable. Most flaws you worry about go unnoticed, and others' feedback is usually more useful than another round of polishing alone.

## Run small experiments
Deliberately do one low-stakes thing imperfectly, such as sending an email without a third proofread, and note what actually happens.

## Separate worth from performance
Your value does not change with each result. Writing down things you did well, and the effort behind them, balances the habit of noticing only what went wrong. If perfectionism is causing distress or paralysis, therapy can help.
