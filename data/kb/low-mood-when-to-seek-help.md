## Normal sadness and something more persistent
Everyone has low days, especially after a setback. It is worth taking seriously when low mood lasts most of the day, nearly every day, for two weeks or more, or when it comes with several of these: losing interest in things you usually enjoy, changes in sleep or appetite, constant tiredness, difficulty concentrating, feeling worthless or guilty, or withdrawing from people.

## This is not a diagnosis
Only a qualified professional can assess what is going on, and many things can cause these symptoms, including physical health problems such as thyroid issues or vitamin deficiencies. A doctor can rule those out.

## What you can do
Tell one person you trust how you have been feeling. Book an appointment with a doctor, psychiatrist or clinical psychologist. If cost or access is a problem, Tele-MANAS (14416) offers free tele-counselling in many languages. Keep up small routines like sleep, meals and a short walk, which support mood without being a cure.

## If you have thoughts of harming yourself
Please contact a helpline or emergency services straight away rather than waiting for an appointment.
