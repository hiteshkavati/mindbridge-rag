title: Dealing with anger and the urge to snap
topics: anger, irritability, conflict, self-control

## Anger is a signal
Anger is often a cover for something underneath: hurt, fear, feeling disrespected or being stretched too thin. It is not the problem in itself; what you do with it is.

## Catch it early
Learn your early signs: a hot face, clenched jaw, faster heartbeat, a tight voice. The earlier you notice, the more options you have.

## Create a gap
Step away if you can ("I need five minutes, then let's continue"). Exhale slowly a few times. Walk, climb stairs or do something physical to burn off the adrenaline. If you are about to send an angry message, write it, do not send it, and look at it again after an hour.

## Come back and speak
Once calmer, name the need underneath: "I was upset because I felt my work was dismissed." State what you want to change and listen to their side.

## When to get help
If anger leads you to hurt people, break things, damage relationships or frightens you, speak to a counsellor or psychologist. Anger management is well suited to therapy.
