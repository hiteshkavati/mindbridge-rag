## How it keeps going
Social anxiety is a strong fear of being judged or embarrassed. Avoiding situations brings short-term relief but confirms the belief that the situations are dangerous. Spotting small "safety behaviours", like rehearsing every sentence or avoiding eye contact, helps too, since they stop you from learning that you can cope without them.

## Build a ladder
List feared situations and rate each from 0 to 100. Start with something around 20 to 30, for example asking a shop assistant a question, and repeat it until it feels easier before moving up: speaking up in a meeting, then introducing yourself at an event.

## Test predictions
Before a situation, write what you fear will happen and how likely it is. Afterwards compare it with what actually happened.

## Shift attention outward
In conversations, focus on what the other person is saying instead of monitoring how you look. Most people are busy with themselves.

## Getting support
Expect discomfort; it is part of the process. CBT is effective for social anxiety. If it limits work, study or relationships significantly, consider seeing a professional.
