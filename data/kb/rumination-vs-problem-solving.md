## Spotting the difference
Problem solving asks "what can I do next?" and ends in an action. Rumination asks "why did this happen to me?" or "what is wrong with me?" and goes round in circles. After ten minutes of rumination you usually feel worse and no closer to an answer.

## Interrupt it
Notice and label it: "I am ruminating." Then change the question from why to what: "What is one thing I could do differently, or one step I can take today?" If there is nothing to do, accept that and move on.

## Change channel
Rumination feeds on idle time. Do something absorbing that uses your hands or body: a walk, cooking, cleaning, a call with a friend, a game. Set a ten-minute limit for thinking it through, ideally with a pen and paper, then stop.

## Avoid reassurance loops
Asking others again and again whether you did the right thing brings relief for a minute and keeps the habit going.

## When to get support
If you cannot stop replaying events for weeks, or it comes with low mood or anxiety, a therapist can teach techniques that work well for rumination.
