## Choose your moment
Raise things when both of you are calm and not rushed, not in the middle of an argument or late at night. Say up front what you want: "Can we talk about how we split chores? I want us both to feel it is fair."

## Use I-statements
Describe your experience instead of accusing: "I felt ignored when the plans changed without a message, and I would like a heads-up next time." Avoid "always" and "never", which invite defence.

## Listen and reflect
Before replying, say back what you heard: "So you felt pressured when I raised it in front of others?" It often lowers the temperature.

## Take breaks
When either of you is flooded (racing heart, raised voices), agree to pause for twenty minutes and come back. Say when you will resume so it does not feel like avoidance.

## Know the limits
If conflict repeats without progress, a couples or family counsellor can help. If you feel afraid of your partner, controlled or unsafe, that is not a communication problem. Reach out to someone you trust or local services, and call 112 if you are in danger.
