---
title: Behavioural activation for low mood
topics: low mood, motivation, depression
---
## The loop
When mood drops, motivation drops, so you do less. Doing less removes the small rewards that lift mood, and mood drops further. Behavioural activation breaks the loop from the action side: you act first and let motivation follow, rather than waiting to feel like it.

## Getting started
Make a short list of activities in three groups: things that give pleasure (a favourite show, a walk, a call with a friend), things that give a sense of achievement (washing the dishes, one email, ten minutes of study), and basics (shower, meals, a regular wake-up time). Schedule one or two for tomorrow at specific times. Start tiny: five minutes counts.

## Track it
Before and after each activity, rate your mood from 0 to 10. People are often surprised that mood improves slightly even when they did not feel like doing it. That evidence is useful on bad days.

## Be realistic
Pick activities that are doable on your worst day. If a low mood has lasted more than a couple of weeks or is getting in the way of daily life, speak to a professional as well. This is a self-help technique, not a replacement for treatment.
