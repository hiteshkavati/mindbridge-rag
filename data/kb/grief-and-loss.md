## There is no schedule
Grief does not move in neat stages. It tends to come in waves, with good hours followed by sudden pain triggered by a song, a smell or a date. Reactions include numbness, anger, guilt, tiredness, trouble concentrating and changes in appetite or sleep. All of these are common.

## Gentle things that help
Keep a few simple routines: meals, sleep, a short walk. Talk about the person with those who knew them, or write to them. Rituals, anniversaries and small acts of remembrance can give the feelings somewhere to go. It is fine to laugh and fine to cry.

## Other kinds of loss
People also grieve a job, a relationship, a pet or a future they planned. These losses are real even when others do not acknowledge them.

## Other people
Choose a few people who can listen without trying to fix things. Support groups can help you feel less alone.

## When to get extra help
If months later you still cannot function, feel stuck in intense pain, or have thoughts of harming yourself or joining the person who died, speak to a professional or helpline soon.
