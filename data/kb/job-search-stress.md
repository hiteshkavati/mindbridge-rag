---
title: Coping with job search stress and rejection
topics: work, job search, rejection, uncertainty
---
## Rejection is information, not a verdict
Most applications get no reply, and many good candidates are rejected for reasons unrelated to ability: timing, an internal candidate, a budget freeze. Reading each rejection as a judgement on your worth is a thinking trap that drains energy.

## Control the process, not the outcome
You cannot control whether a company says yes, but you can control how many tailored applications you send, how many people you message, and how much time you spend improving a skill. Set weekly targets for those and judge your week by them.

## Keep a structure
Fixed working hours for the search, a daily walk or exercise, a regular sleep schedule, and at least one thing in the day that has nothing to do with jobs. Unstructured days feed anxiety.

## Watch the comparison loop
Scrolling through other people's announcements makes your own situation look worse. Limit that time and mute accounts that leave you feeling small.

## Money worries and low mood
Write down your actual financial runway so the fear has a number. If low mood, poor sleep or hopelessness build up, talk to someone, since a long search is a real strain.
