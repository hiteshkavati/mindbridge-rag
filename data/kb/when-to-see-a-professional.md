## Signs it is time
Symptoms last more than a couple of weeks, they interfere with study, work or relationships, you are using alcohol or other substances to cope, you have physical symptoms with no clear cause, or you have thoughts of harming yourself. You do not need to be in crisis to ask for help.

## Who does what
A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can diagnose and prescribe medication. A clinical psychologist provides assessment and therapy; in India, check that they are registered with the Rehabilitation Council of India. A counsellor offers talking support for everyday problems and may be based at a college or workplace. A general doctor is a fine first step and can rule out physical causes.

## Finding access
Tele-MANAS (14416) is free, runs 24x7 and can refer you onward. Many colleges and some employers offer counselling. NGOs and teaching hospitals often provide lower-cost sessions.

## What to expect
The first session is mostly about telling your story and agreeing goals. It can take a few sessions to know whether the fit is right, and it is fine to switch therapist. Therapy tends to work gradually, and homework between sessions helps.
