## What it is
Self-compassion means treating yourself the way you would treat a friend in the same situation. Researchers who study it, such as Kristin Neff, describe three parts: noticing your pain without drowning in it, remembering that struggle is part of being human, and responding with kindness instead of attack.

## The myth
Many people believe harsh self-criticism keeps them motivated. Studies tend to find the opposite: people who are kinder to themselves after a failure recover faster and are more willing to try again, while harsh self-judgement raises fear of failing.

## Try this
When you catch the inner critic, ask: "What would I say to a close friend who made this mistake?" Then say that to yourself, ideally in writing. Add a line acknowledging the difficulty ("this is hard and it makes sense I feel bad"). Put a hand on your chest or take a slow breath if it helps.

## Note
It can feel awkward at first, and that is normal. If self-criticism is relentless, tied to feeling worthless, or linked to depression, a therapist can help.
