## What it does
Grounding exercises pull your attention out of racing thoughts and into what your senses are picking up right now. The 5-4-3-2-1 version is popular because it needs no equipment and can be done anywhere, including on a bus or at your desk.

## How to do it
Look around and name five things you can see. Then four things you can physically feel (your feet on the floor, the chair under you, the fabric of your shirt). Then three things you can hear, two you can smell, and one you can taste. Go slowly and add detail: not just "a cup" but its colour, where the light hits it.

## If it feels pointless at first
That is common. It works better if you practise it on an ordinary day, so it is familiar when you need it. Other quick grounding options are pressing your feet firmly into the floor, naming objects in the room by colour, or counting backwards from 100 in sevens. If you still feel flooded after ten minutes, pair it with slow breathing or talk to someone nearby.
