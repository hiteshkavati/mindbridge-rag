## Thoughts are not facts
When we are stressed, the mind produces fast, harsh interpretations. A thought record, a basic tool from cognitive behavioural therapy, slows that down so you can test the thought instead of just believing it.

## The steps
Write down the situation (what happened, where, with whom). Write the automatic thought ("I messed up the interview, I will never get a job"). Note the emotion and rate it from 0 to 100. List the evidence for the thought and the evidence against it. Then write a more balanced version and re-rate the emotion.

## Common thinking traps
Catastrophising (jumping to the worst case), mind reading (assuming you know what others think), all-or-nothing thinking, fortune telling, and "should" statements. Naming the trap often takes some of its force away.

## What balanced means
It is not forced positivity. A balanced thought for the example above might be: "One interview went badly. I can review what to prepare differently, and one result does not predict the rest." The aim is accuracy, not cheerfulness.
