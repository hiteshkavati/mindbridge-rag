## What it looks like
Burnout is usually described as three things together: deep exhaustion that rest does not fix, growing cynicism or detachment from work, and a feeling that you are no longer effective at it. You may dread Monday, snap at colleagues, or feel nothing about work that once mattered to you.

## Common causes
Long-term overload, unclear expectations, little control over your work, lack of recognition, and an always-on culture where messages arrive at all hours.

## Recovery steps
Start with the basics: protect sleep, take real breaks during the day, and use your leave. Look for small changes in workload you can negotiate, such as pushing a deadline or dropping a low-value meeting. Reconnect with something outside work that is not productive on purpose. Talk to your manager with specific examples rather than general complaints.

## When it is more than burnout
Burnout and depression overlap. If you feel hopeless, lose interest in everything and not just work, or have trouble functioning at home, see a professional. If the job itself is the problem and does not change, planning a move may be part of recovery.
