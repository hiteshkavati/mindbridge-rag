## What it is
Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR) means tensing and then releasing muscle groups in sequence. The contrast makes tension easier to notice, and releasing it signals the body that it can settle.

## Steps
Sit or lie comfortably. Starting at your feet, tense the muscles gently for about five seconds (curl your toes), then release suddenly and notice the difference for ten to fifteen seconds. Move upwards: calves, thighs, stomach, hands, arms, shoulders, face. Breathe normally; do not hold your breath. A full round takes ten to fifteen minutes.

## Tips
Tense to about 70 percent effort, not maximum. Skip any area that is injured or painful. It works best with regular practice, for example once a day for two weeks, and is useful at bedtime. Some people find it easier with a recording guiding them.

## Limits
PMR reduces body tension and helps many people feel calmer, but it is not a treatment for anxiety disorders or depression. If tension and worry are constant, talk to a professional as well.
